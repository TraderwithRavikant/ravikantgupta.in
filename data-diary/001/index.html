How often does NIFTY's opening gap fill by the close?

Small gaps usually close. Big gaps usually don't.

Data: my one-minute NIFTY futures data, near-month contract, 3 Aug 2018 to 25 Aug 2026

Across 1,980 sessions since August 2018, NIFTY traded back to the previous day's close on 64% of them. But that average hides almost everything. Gaps under 0.25% filled 82% of the time. Gaps of 1% or more filled only 19% of the time.

Up or down, almost the same

Gap-ups and gap-downs behaved almost the same: 65% and 63% filled. The one difference was in the biggest gaps. Gap-downs of 1% or more filled 15% of the time and gap-ups 26%, but those are small groups (75 and 54 days), so I wouldn't lean on that gap.

When the fill happens

When a gap did fill, it usually filled early.

  • Half of all fills had happened by 09:22.
  • 38% happened in the very first minute. 93% of those were gaps under 0.25%.
  • 73% had happened by 10:15. Only 6% came after 14:00.
  • For gaps of 1% or more, the typical fill came much later, at 11:17.

What it means for a trader

"Gaps always fill" is half true. It is mostly true for small gaps and mostly false for big ones.

So if a plan depends on a gap filling, the size of the gap is the first thing to check, before the trade and not after. And most of the fills that do happen come in the first hour.

This is a description of how the market behaved. It is not a signal and not a strategy. I haven't tested whether trading it would make money after costs. That is a different question.

Also in the data

  • Year to year, the rate moved between 52% and 75%. Years with bigger gaps tended to fill less. In 2020 the typical gap was 0.40%; in 2025 it was 0.16%. But size doesn't explain all of the movement, so one year's number is not a rule.
  • Monday gaps filled less often. These are the gaps that come after a weekend. For gaps of 0.25% or more, Mondays filled 36% of the time, against 40% to 55% on other days. This deserves its own entry before I say more.
  • The largest gaps all came in March and April 2020. The biggest was −10.2% on 23 March 2020, and it didn't fill.

Method

  • Data. My own one-minute NIFTY futures data. There is no spot data in the dataset, so the near-month monthly future stands in for the index.
  • Previous close. The last trade of the same futures contract in the previous session. Using the same contract matters on the day after expiry: comparing the new month's open with the old month's close would create a fake gap from the difference in their premiums.
  • Open. The first trade of the 09:15 one-minute bar.
  • Filled. For a gap up, price traded down to the previous close at any minute before the close. For a gap down, it traded up to it.
  • Sample. 1,983 regular sessions from 3 Aug 2018 to 25 Aug 2026. Three opened exactly flat, which leaves 1,980 gaps.
  • Left out (13 days). The first day of the data, which has no previous close; eight Diwali Muhurat sessions; the NSE outage day (24 Feb 2021); two short special Saturday sessions (2 Mar and 18 May 2024); and 6 Jan 2020, where the data starts at 09:27. Each one is listed with its reason in the downloadable numbers.
  • Checks. Seven days were recomputed straight from the raw files, including 13 March 2020 and 3 June 2024. All matched. On every roll day, the previous close came from a 15:29 trade, so none used a stale price.

Limitations

  • Futures, not the index. Futures carry a premium and trade on their own. The NIFTY index itself may behave a little differently.
  • The last trade, not the official close. NSE's official close is an average of the last half hour. I use the last trade at 15:29 so that every day is measured the same way.
  • One index, one period. August 2018 to August 2026, including the 2020 crash. The past is not a promise about the future.
  • "Filled" only means touched. It says nothing about what price did next, or whether anyone made money from it.
  • Extra bars in recent files. From August 2026 the data has extra bars between 15:30 and 15:39, which I ignored. Keeping them changes 16 days and moves the overall rate from 64.2% to 64.1%.
