The lab

Concept, then test

An idea from the books on my shelf, written as a precise rule, then tested on my own data with every cost included. The result goes up whether the idea holds or not.

Lab #01: The trend-following pullback

Trade only in the direction of the trend, and only after price has come back to a fair level instead of chasing it.

Topic: Trend & setups · Source: Alexander Elder's "value zone", The New Trading for a Living

What a complete rule needs

Five parts, or it isn't a system

  1. Market & timeframeWhat I trade, and on which chart. Decided once.
  2. SetupThe condition that makes a trade possible at all.
  3. Entry triggerThe exact event that says "now".
  4. Stop & exitWhere I'm wrong, and how I get out when I'm right.
  5. Position sizeHow much, worked out from the stop, never from hope.

miss one, and the market fills the gap with your emotions

How every test is run

  1. The rule is written before the test. Every condition is precise enough for a computer to follow.
  2. The data is split. The rule is built on the first 70% of the data, then run unchanged on the last 30%, which it has never seen.
  3. Every cost is charged. Brokerage, STT, exchange charges, GST and slippage.
  4. Small samples are called small. Fewer than 100 trades means the result is weak, and the entry says so.
  5. The drawdown sits next to the result. Never below it, never left out.
  6. No re-tuning after the test. If the rule fails on the data it has never seen, it failed, and it goes up as a failure.

Seedha baat: a test tells you whether an idea held up in the past. It promises nothing about the future.
